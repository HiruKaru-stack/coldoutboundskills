# Prospect Qualification Methodology — "Can this company afford our premium outbound service?"

> Preparation document for building a qualification skill.
> Captures the full method and all decision rules agreed in the session dated 2026-09-05 → 2026-09-07.

---

## 1. Purpose

We have a **premium, high-priced outbound (cold email) service** run by a **small/boutique agency**. Reference price point discussed: **~$1,600/month**.

We are **not** qualifying on ICP, industry, geography, employee size, or keywords — that filtering is already done when the list is built. This methodology is the **final manual check**: go to the company's website (plus a few external lookups) and decide whether they are a good fit for us to contact and sell to.

**The one thing that matters:** *Can this company afford a premium outbound retainer, and would it make sense for them to buy one?*

Everything below is a way of answering that from public information.

---

## 2. The core question

> **Does this company sell services/products that are expensive enough (mid‑ticket or higher, not low‑ticket) that they have the margin and the mindset to pay a premium outbound agency — and is there actually outbound work to do for them?**

If yes, and none of the hard disqualifiers apply → **SELECT**.

---

## 3. Decision rules (LOCKED)

### 3.1 Hard disqualifiers — any one of these = **SKIP**

| # | Rule | Detail |
|---|------|--------|
| **D1** | **A real parent above it puts the buying decision out of reach** | The rule exists for **one reason**: if the ownership/decision sits above the company, we can't contact and sell to that specific company. Apply it by that reason, not by legal form. **SKIP** if owned by another **operating company**, a **larger / branded group** ("part of the X Group", "a X company", group management, several operating companies), or a **PE / VC / investment fund** with a controlling or significant stake. **NOT a skip (the exception):** a **pure holding shell owned by the target's own founders / operators as individuals** — a personal / family / tax holdco or MBO vehicle ("[Founder] Holding / Ventures / Invest s.r.o.", "X Holding ApS") with no operations, whose only real asset is this company (± the same founders' other small ventures), and no group branding on the website. Same people, same reachable decision-maker → treat as **independent**. **Test:** trace the holdco up one level — the target's own founders as individuals → not D1; a different company / fund / outside people → D1 → SKIP. A **founder's firsthand "they're a fit" always overrides** this proxy. |
| **D2** | **Owns subcompanies itself — this is FINE** | If the target company sits at the top and has its **own smaller companies/subsidiaries beneath it**, that is **not** a problem. Being a parent is fine; being a child is not. |
| **D3** | **Something on the site is offered explicitly FREE** | SKIP if the site offers a **free** demo, **free** trial, **free** consultation / strategy call / audit / workshop, a **free plan / freemium**, "start free", "sign up free", "no credit card required", or self‑serve signup. **NOT D3 (keep):** a plain form to fill out for a consultation, a demo, or contact when it is *not* advertised as free — "Book a demo", "Request a demo", "Book a consultation", "Contact us", "Get a quote" are normal B2B sales entry points. **Test:** is the word **free** (zdarma / gratis / bezplatná / "no cost" / "no obligation, free") attached? Yes → SKIP. Just a form, no "free" → fine. *(Session‑3 narrowing — was previously "any free demo/trial/consultation".)* |
| **D4** | **No B2B sales motion** | SKIP if the company sells nothing to other businesses — proprietary trading firms, funds trading their own capital, pure holding companies, internal‑only operations — and the main call‑to‑action on the site is "Join us" / careers. There is no outbound work to sell them, regardless of how profitable they are. |
| **D5** | **Core business is IT staffing / body‑leasing / contractor resourcing** | SKIP if their primary offering is supplying people/developers billed on day rates (staff augmentation, "body leasing", contractor placement). Commoditised, thin‑margin, and they typically run large in‑house BD teams. *(Note: a company that does real project work and also offers some staff augmentation on the side is not auto‑skipped — judge the core business.)* |
| **D6** | **Clearly low‑ticket / self‑serve** | SKIP if their own services are clearly cheap: transparent low public pricing (e.g. tens to low‑hundreds per month), instant credit‑card checkout, high‑volume low‑price model. |
| **D7** | **Customers are purely micro / solopreneur / consumer** | SKIP if all their customers are consumers (B2C), freelancers/solopreneurs, or micro‑businesses (1–5 person operations: local café, plumber, single‑location salon). Their own service is almost certainly cheap and the model can't absorb a premium retainer. **Mid‑market customers are fine** (see 3.3). |
| **D8** | **Customer base is EXCLUSIVELY government / public sector** | SKIP only if the company sells **essentially only** to ministries, state agencies, public hospitals, universities, state‑owned enterprises, municipalities, defence/law‑enforcement/intelligence — with **no meaningful private commercial customers**. Reason: **we can't deliver results for a government‑only client base** — those buyers are reached via public tenders and procurement, not cold email; cycles 6–18 months. **Government being the MAJORITY of the client base is NOT a skip.** If the company also genuinely serves private enterprises — any private companies, corporate/works canteens, private industry — then D8 does **not** fire; run outbound at the private side. Case studies featuring government bodies are not enough on their own to skip — check whether private companies are also served (site "…for hospitals, schools **and companies**", private client refs, corporate/works‑canteen use, etc.). Use the CZ/SK public‑contracts registry (smlouvy.gov.cz, hlidacstatu.cz) to size the government share; then check the site/references for private clients. |
| **D9** | **Competitor — the company itself sells outbound / cold email / lead generation / appointment‑setting / SDR‑as‑a‑service** | SKIP if their service menu includes cold email, outbound calling campaigns, lead generation, appointment setting / "meetings on your calendar", demand generation, or SDR/BDR outsourcing. Includes cold‑email agencies, lead‑gen agencies, demand‑gen agencies, appointment‑setting firms, sales‑development agencies, and BPO / call centres whose menu lists "outbound campaigns" or "leads". Reason: they run outbound in‑house at scale and will never buy an outbound retainer — and they are a direct competitor. Watch for: *outbound, outboundové kampaně, lead generation, generování leadů, appointment setting, obchodní schůzky, sales development, demand gen*. |
| **D10** | **Core offering is geospatial / GIS / mapping / surveying — outbound isn't a viable channel** | SKIP if the company's headline business is geographic/spatial: aerial or satellite imaging, orthophoto, photogrammetry, LiDAR, geodesy, land surveying, cadastre / land‑registry work, cartography, GIS software / platforms / data, mobile mapping, 3D terrain or city models, urban‑planning (územní plán) documentation, or spatial analytics. Reason: the buyer universe is tiny, heavily municipal / regional / public‑infrastructure, and tender‑driven (6–18 mo RFP cycles); the purchase is infrequent and technical (surveyors + GIS staff + procurement, not one reachable growth buyer); there is no repeatable outreach → demo → deal motion. Fires **regardless of ownership, ticket size, or client mix** — even if some clients are private (that's what separates it from D8, which is about the client mix). A freemium GIS web‑portal alongside the surveying business does not rescue it. Watch for: *geodézie, mapování, ortofoto, letecké/družicové snímkování, fotogrammetrie, LiDAR, kataster, GIS, územní plán, mobilní mapování, 3D model, prostorová analytika*. |

### 3.2 SELECT criteria — must clear **all** of these

1. **Independent for selling purposes** — no operating parent, branded group, or PE/VC/investment fund above it. A holding shell owned by the target's own founders/operators (same reachable decision-maker) is fine (D1).
2. **No free demo / trial / consultation** (D3).
3. **Real B2B sales motion** — sells a service or product to other businesses (D4).
4. **Not low‑ticket** — deals are custom‑quoted, no cheap self‑serve pricing; mid‑ticket or higher (D6).
5. **Customers are mid‑market or larger** — not purely micro/solopreneur/consumer (D7).
6. **Not a government‑only client base** — some genuine private‑enterprise customers exist (D8). Government can be the majority; it just can't be the entire client base.
7. **The work has real substance** — custom engineering, integration, specialised software, data/analytics, automation, or strategic depth. Exclude only genuinely "easy" commodity work (template websites, basic SEO packages, logo/graphic design).
8. **Not a competitor** — the company does not itself sell outbound / cold email / lead generation / appointment‑setting / SDR‑as‑a‑service (D9).
9. **Not a geospatial / GIS / mapping / surveying company** (D10) — the service category has a tiny, mostly‑public, tender‑driven buyer universe cold outbound can't address.

If it clears all nine and trips none of D1–D10 → **SELECT**.

### 3.3 What we do **NOT** filter on

- **Company size / employee count / revenue size** — NEVER a disqualifier, in either direction. Not "too big", not "too small", not "too little revenue to afford our retainer". The list is already size‑filtered. A 5‑person agency on €250k revenue that serves mid‑ticket clients and is reachable is a SELECT (Studio 9, session 3). Do **not** let a small revenue figure become an implied D6 — D6 is about *their pricing model* (cheap public pricing / self‑serve checkout / micro‑only clients), not their size.
- **Funding stage** — not a disqualifier. A funded startup with a mid‑ticket product can still be a SELECT if it clears 3.2.
- **Enterprise clients are a bonus, not a requirement** — mid‑market customers (roughly 50–500 employees, real revenue: an €80M manufacturer, a regional retail chain, an established B2B distributor) are perfectly fine.
- **Industry / vertical** — no industry filter.
- **Geography** — no geography filter.

### 3.4 "Depends" service categories — judge case by case, do **not** auto‑skip

These came up and are **not** blanket disqualifiers. Run them through 3.1 + 3.2:

- E‑commerce / online‑store build agencies (custom e‑shops for retailers)
- MSP / IT infrastructure / helpdesk / hardware reselling
- Generic marketing / SEO / branding / design agencies
- Mobile‑app development shops
- General custom‑software development shops (mixed client base)

A company in one of these categories is a **SELECT** if it is independent, has no free demo/consultation, sells to mid‑market or larger businesses, isn't low‑ticket, and the work has real substance. It's a **SKIP** if it fails any of those — most often because of a free‑consultation hook, a parent company, or genuinely commodity/cheap work.

## 4. Signal reference — how to read a website

### 4.1 Positive signals (points toward SELECT)

**Pricing & commercial model**
- No public pricing, or "Custom" / "Contact us" / "Request a quote" / "Get a calculation"
- Annual contracts, "minimum X seats", quotes in the thousands+
- Consultative language: "scoping call", "custom proposal"

**Sales motion**
- Primary CTA is "Contact Sales" / "Talk to an expert" / "Request a demo" routed through a qualifying form
- There is a sales team to talk to; regional sales contacts; a `obchod@` / `sales@` address

**Who their customers are**
- Named client logos / "Trusted by" wall — recognisable brands, or credible mid‑market names
- Case studies describing scale — large deployments, multi‑year, "$XM saved", "rolled out to N sites/countries"
- Testimonials from senior titles (VP, CIO, Head of, Director)
- A dedicated "Enterprise" plan or "For Enterprise" page
- Serves regulated / heavy industries: financial services, healthcare, insurance, energy, manufacturing, government, telco, logistics/postal

**Product / service complexity**
- Cloud infrastructure, data platforms, AI/ML platforms, cybersecurity, ERP/MES, capital‑markets software, industrial automation & systems integration, specialised consulting (data/BI, actuarial, regulatory, digital transformation)
- Implies long implementation: "onboarding team", "professional services", "solution architects", "dedicated CSM"

**Trust / procurement infrastructure** (enterprises force vendors to build these)
- SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 9001, HIPAA, PCI‑DSS, GDPR, FedRAMP badges; a "Trust Center" / "Security" page
- SSO / SAML / SCIM, RBAC, audit logs, on‑prem / private‑cloud deployment
- SLA, uptime guarantee, MSA / DPA, "we respond to security questionnaires", RFP language

**Maturity & credibility**
- Established (5+ years), multiple offices, press/newsroom, awards, analyst recognition (Gartner/Forrester/G2), Deloitte Fast 50 etc.
- Technology partnerships: Microsoft Gold/Solutions Partner, SAP, IBM, AWS, UiPath, Siemens, FANUC, SICK, etc.

**Hiring signals** (careers page / LinkedIn)
- Enterprise / Strategic AE, Field Sales, Sales Engineer, Solutions Consultant, Partnerships Manager, SDR/BDR roles
- Implementation, Customer Success, Professional Services, Solutions Architect roles

**Positioning language**
- ROI, TCO, business outcomes, risk reduction, compliance, scale, mission‑critical, "built for the enterprise"
- Named partner ecosystem (AWS Advanced Partner, Salesforce ISV, "Deloitte/Accenture implementation partners")

### 4.2 Negative signals (points toward SKIP)

- Free trial / freemium / "free plan" / "start free" as the main path
- Transparent low pricing (tens–low‑hundreds per month) with instant self‑serve checkout
- "No credit card required" / month‑to‑month / "cancel anytime"
- Free consultation / free audit / free strategy call as the hook
- App‑store / marketplace plugin (Shopify app, WordPress plugin, Chrome extension)
- Commodity services: basic web dev, template sites, generic SEO, logo/brand design, VA services
- E‑commerce / DTC selling physical products to consumers
- Local services business; one‑person / tiny shop with no company substance
- Customers shown are all freelancers, coaches, startups, micro‑businesses
- Main CTA is "Join us" / careers, with nothing sold to businesses

### 4.3 Disqualifier‑hunting (be thorough — these are easy to miss)

- **Parent company (D1):** footer copyright a different entity; "a [X] company"; "part of the [Y] Group"; "backed by [holding co]"; "acquired by …" in the news; brand is one of several under a portfolio site. **Almost always needs an external lookup** — see §5.2.
- **Own group vs owned by a group:** "[Brand] Group" with subsidiaries listed *beneath* it is fine (D2). Being *inside* someone else's group is not (D1).
- **Free demo/consultation (D3):** check the hero, the nav, the pricing page, the contact page, and any pop‑up. Often phrased as "Book a free consultation", "Get a free audit", "konzultácia zdarma", "nezáväzná konzultácia".
- **No B2B motion (D4):** if you can't find anything they sell to other businesses and the site is basically a recruiting page → SKIP.

## 5. Where to look

### 5.1 Pages to check on the site (every time)

1. Homepage — hero, primary CTA
2. `/pricing` (or equivalent) — custom vs cheap self‑serve
3. `/customers`, `/references`, `/case-studies`, `/portfolio` — customer size and type
4. `/security`, `/trust`, `/compliance` — procurement infrastructure
5. **Footer** — parent company, copyright entity, "a X company", group branding
6. `/about`, `/company`, `/o-nas` — history, ownership, group structure
7. `/careers`, `/jobs` — sales roles, seniority
8. **Both language versions** — most CEE sites have an English + local‑language site and **they differ**; content (e.g. the word "Enterprise") can be on one and not the other.

### 5.2 External lookups (required for the parent‑company check)

The website almost never states its owner. Check:

- **LinkedIn company page** — headcount (most reliable single source), "part of" / affiliated pages
- **Crunchbase** — acquisitions, parent org, investors
- Local company registry:
  - Czech Republic: `justice.cz`, `rejstrik-firem.kurzy.cz`, `or.justice.cz`
  - Slovakia: `orsr.sk`, `finstat.sk`, `aobp.cz`
  - Denmark: `cvr.dk`, `proff.dk`, `virk.dk`
  - UK: Companies House (`find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk`)
- Company profile aggregators: `dnb.com`, `emis.com`, `zoominfo.com`, `apollo.io` (treat as secondary — often stale)
- Press: search `"[company]" acquired OR "part of" OR "becomes part of" OR group`

### 5.3 Data cautions

- The page‑to‑text reader can **drop content** — if a key page looks empty or a claim seems missing, fetch the specific sub‑page and the other language version before concluding.
- **Employee counts disagree** across sources (seen: 10–19 vs 10–50 for the same company). Prefer LinkedIn.
- **Anonymised client lists** ("a Swiss bank", "an Austrian insurer") can't be verified — treat as weak evidence, not proof. Still counts toward "sells to mid‑market or larger", but note it as unverified.
- **Thin sites** — if there genuinely isn't enough information to judge, output **"INSUFFICIENT DATA — manual check needed"**, do not guess.
- Registry data on ownership can lag reorganisations (a sister company may have been sold; a group may have been dissolved) — cross‑check the date.

## 6. Output format (use every time)

**Rules:** every signal names **where it was found** (exact page URL, or the external source) and its **effect** (include / exclude / flag + which rule Dx). All sources in **English** (or English translation + the URL + how to auto‑translate). End with a one‑line verdict.

```
COMPANY: <name> — <URL>

| Signal | Where found | Effect (rule) |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| <what the site/lookup shows> | <exact URL or source> | include / exclude / flag (Dx or §3.2.n) |
| ... | ... | ... |

SUMMARY
VERDICT: SELECT | SKIP | MANUAL CHECK
REASON: <1–2 sentences tied to the rules>, each claim with (source: <where>)
IF MANUAL CHECK: <exactly what to verify, which English page/field, how to translate it>
```

Signals to cover every time: what they sell + ticket level; who their customers are (type/size, private vs government); free demo/trial/consultation (checked across home, about, pricing, references, careers, contact, footer, both languages); parent/holding company above them; whether they own subsidiaries (not a problem); B2B sales motion / main CTA; real substance in the work.

## 8. Notable calibration points from the session

- **The parent‑company rule is the single biggest filter and the easiest to get wrong.** It nearly always requires an external registry / LinkedIn / Crunchbase lookup — the website won't tell you. A company owning its *own* subsidiaries does not count (D2).
- **D1 REFINED (session 3): it is about *reachability of the buyer*, not legal form.** SKIP only when a real parent owns the decision — an operating company, a branded group, or a PE/VC/investment fund (Synerga → AVANT ENERGY SICAV fund; Controlis → Liftrock group; Senvio → MIBCON Group; Meonzi → "a Trask Company"; Sophia → SUDOP CIT; Shopsys → ABUGO; ANETE → TTC group; Medicalc → Infini One a.s.). **A holding shell owned by the target's own founders/operators is NOT D1** — same reachable decision‑maker → treat as independent. UX Fans (→ UXF Ventures s.r.o., the founders' own vehicle) = SELECT. **This reverses the earlier NEOXX and Datacon SKIPs** — Aterea s.r.o. (NEOXX's two managers' MBO vehicle) and LHUB Holding ApS (Datacon owner's personal holdco) were founder wrappers, not real parents. Always trace the holdco up one level: reach the target's founders as individuals → keep; a different company / fund / outside people → skip. And a **founder's firsthand call that a company is a fit overrides the registry proxy.**
- **"Enterprise‑only" was relaxed to "mid‑market or larger."** Do not skip a company just because its clients are mid‑market rather than Fortune‑500 / ASX‑200.
- **"Too big" was dropped. "Too small" was dropped (session 3).** No headcount, revenue, or funding ceiling OR floor. Never SKIP because a company "looks too small to afford the retainer", and never turn a small/declining revenue figure into an implied D6. The three real checks: (1) can we reach the decision‑maker (no real parent + a findable contact); (2) do their case studies show mid‑ and high‑ticket clients; (3) any hard disqualifier (D3 free offer / D9 competitor / D5 body‑shop / D8 government‑only / D4 no B2B). Studio 9 (~5 staff, €250k rev, serves TON/Meopta/Janošík) = SELECT. MakeBeCool was skipped partly on revenue — under this rule that reasoning is invalid; it would turn on client calibre alone.
- **The word "Enterprise" on a site is not proof of anything.** On one company it meant "enterprise ERP systems we integrate with", not "we sell to enterprises". Read what it actually refers to.
- **Free consultation is a real disqualifier** (D3) — this is what removed NDC. Watch for "free audit", "free strategy call", "free workshop", "konzultácia zdarma", "bezplatná konzultácia". **BUT session‑3 narrowing: D3 is ONLY about the word "free".** A plain "Book a demo" / "Request a demo" / "Book a consultation" / contact form that is NOT called free is **fine** — keep it. GoodVision skipped on an explicit "Free trial" CTA; a bare "Book a demo" would not have.
- **Prop trading / own‑capital / no‑customers businesses** are a clean SKIP even though they're rich (D4).
- **Staffing / body‑leasing as the core business** is a clean SKIP (D5); a project company that *also* offers staff augmentation is not.
- **D8 fires ONLY on a government‑ONLY client base** — not for affordability reasons but because a small outbound agency cannot prospect government buyers (tenders, procurement, 6–18 month cycles). **Government being the majority is fine** as long as private enterprises are also genuinely served (corporate/works canteens, private industry, named private clients) → run outbound at the private side. Don't skip off government‑heavy case studies alone — check for private clients first. Cogniware (government/law‑enforcement/intelligence only) and Beset (ministries/state bodies only) were true government‑only → SKIP. ANETE has heavy state‑hospital + Ministry‑of‑Defence use but also private works canteens (ŽĎAS, KNL Catering) → D8 clears (it skipped on D1 instead).
- **Niche infrastructure / hardware sold through a partner or integrator channel — check VERY thoroughly and flag it to the user; not an auto‑SKIP.** When the offering is specialised physical infrastructure or hardware+software (parking / access‑control systems, industrial gates, metering, kiosks, etc.) and the company reaches end‑buyers largely via resellers / integrators / a partner network, the direct outreach → demo → deal motion is weakened — the reachable direct buyer list is small and often project/tender‑driven. Read the whole services + references + go‑to‑market story, give the user a firm lean‑SELECT / lean‑SKIP read with the reason, and let them decide (they know whether they can source prospects for it). Only D10 (geospatial / GIS) is a hard category auto‑SKIP. *(Green Center / green.cz — parking + access‑control systems, partner network in 25+ countries: model recommended SKIP, user chose KEEP, 2026‑09‑09.)*
- **D10 — geospatial / GIS / mapping / surveying is a clean SKIP (session 3), no matter how independent, high‑ticket, or private‑client the company looks.** The *service category* itself can't be sold via cold outbound: buyer universe is a few hundred mostly‑public organisations, purchases are tender‑driven and years apart, and the decision sits with surveyors + GIS staff + procurement. This is different from D8 (client mix) — D10 fires on the *offering*. **TopGis** (topgis.cz — aerial photography, orthophoto, mobile mapping, thermal imaging, GisOnline portal, 3D územní plán, WMS) was flipped SELECT → SKIP. Also flips the earlier **Lutra Consulting** (custom QGIS engineering) and **ARCDATA PRAHA** (national Esri/ArcGIS distributor + GIS consulting) SELECTs.
- **Every signal in the output must cite where it was found** — the exact page URL or the external source — and say which rule it triggers (include / exclude / flag). End every evaluation with a one‑line verdict: SELECT / SKIP / MANUAL CHECK + reason + source.
- **Sources must be given in English** — English‑language sources where possible; otherwise the English translation of the exact text plus the page URL and how to auto‑translate it (Chrome → right‑click → Translate).
